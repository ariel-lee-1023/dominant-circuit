# Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person

Source: Harry G. Frankfurt. Conceptual notes restored and revised from the repository's
historical Stage 0 corpus. These notes summarize the resource; they are not exact
quotations or a fresh verification of the source text.

The current protocol is [Stage 0](../SKILL.md). This resource is optional; it
does not add a mandatory method sequence or a new solver capability.

## Concepts available for investigation

Frankfurt's 1971 article distinguishes wanting to do something (a first-order
desire), wanting to have a desire (a second-order desire), and wanting a particular
desire to govern action (a second-order volition). These are distinctions in his
philosophical account, not clinical or software classifications of a user.

The distinction can help discuss a conflict when a person explicitly wants to
reflect on what should guide a decision. Strength of expression alone does not
show whether someone intends a preference as a scenario, a decision-specific
choice, or a standing commitment.

## Current Stage 0 application

Use this resource only when reflective preference discussion is useful to the
person. Useful decision assistance does not require a conceptual quiz, a second-order
volition declaration, or a standing identity commitment.

Record the scope the participant actually gives a preference using the shared
preference and provenance records. Clear decision-specific adoption remains enough
for that scope; do not ask for repeated blanket agreement. A revised goal is a goal
revision, not evidence that a causal account was falsified.

Keep participants distinct. A manager's throughput goal and an employee's concern
about repeat contacts or workload can remain in conflict. Neither authority nor a
philosophical interpretation creates consensus or invalidates reported evidence.
Unknown access and authority remain unknown. Approval to explore a working account
does not authorize collecting records or changing anyone's workflow.
