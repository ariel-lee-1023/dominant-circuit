# The Book of Why: The New Science of Cause and Effect

Source: Judea Pearl and Dana Mackenzie. Conceptual notes restored and revised from the repository's
historical Stage 0 corpus. These notes summarize the resource; they are not exact
quotations or a fresh verification of the source text.

The current protocol is [Stage 0](../SKILL.md). This resource is optional; it
does not add a mandatory method sequence or a new solver capability.

## Concepts available for investigation

Association, intervention and counterfactual questions ask different things.
An observed association does not by itself supply an intervention effect. Causal
reasoning needs explicit assumptions about how the variables relate and about
what is observed or selected into the sample.

A directed causal graph can make proposed relationships and omissions visible.
Chains, common causes and colliders illustrate why conditioning on another variable
can have different implications. A mediator's role depends on the target effect.
Back-door and front-door reasoning concern identification under specified assumptions;
they do not establish that a proposed graph describes the world.

Selection into ticket logs, changed definitions of resolution time, or missing
repeat contacts can undermine an apparent comparison. Preserve the raw episode
and measurement context before deciding how it fits the graph.

## Current Stage 0 application

Use causal analysis when the question calls for it. The host may propose an initial
graph or a plain-language account. Users may challenge it through an episode,
omitted factor or uncertainty without learning graph notation.

Before referring an identification question to an appropriate method, record the
target quantity, model or graph, observable set, assumptions and exact evidence
revisions. The current library has no identification verifier. Its labels are
qualitative, unresolved or external_unverified; filling these fields cannot earn a
formally checked status. External results require their own traceable analysis.

This narrative source is a conceptual resource, not executable theorem validation.
The prior notes' informal transcription of do-calculus is not retained as a
certificate-producing algorithm. Consult primary formal sources and an appropriate
implementation for an actual identification analysis.

A revised boundary or measurement invalidates dependent interpretations. A failure
to establish identification here does not prove that the effect is unidentifiable
under every possible design or newly available source.
